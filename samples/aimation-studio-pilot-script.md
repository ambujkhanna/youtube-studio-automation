## Working title

**AI Agents vs Chatbots: What's Different?**

Thumbnail/cover text: **THINKS vs DOES**

## Concept

A 40–45 second educational Short. Alex and Mia explain the difference between a chatbot and an AI agent using a simple everyday example. The visual direction assumes the approved polished 3D CGI style: recurring characters, expressive facial animation, physical gestures, animated classroom/lab environment, camera movement, and scene transitions.

## Hook options

1. **Mia:** "A chatbot answers your question. But can it actually do the work?"
2. **Alex:** "What if AI didn't just answer you — it completed the task?"
3. **Mia:** "This is the difference between asking AI and giving AI a job."

Recommended: option 1. It establishes a clear contrast and opens a question immediately. These are editorial judgments, not measured performance predictions; the external hook-scoring utility has not been executed on the user's Windows PC.

## Dialogue and shot plan

| Time | Visual direction | Dialogue / sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Fast push-in. Mia turns toward camera, raises an eyebrow. Alex holds a glowing question card. | **Mia:** "A chatbot answers your question. But can it actually do the work?" |
| 3–7s | Alex taps the card. A chat bubble appears; camera pans to a simple task board. | **Alex:** "A chatbot mainly responds to what you ask." |
| 7–12s | Mia gestures. A small animated assistant window displays a clear text answer. | **Mia:** "Ask it for a travel checklist, and it gives you the list." |
| 12–18s | Transition through the task board. Alex points to three connected steps: plan, check, organise. | **Alex:** "An AI agent can take a goal, plan steps, and use tools it's allowed to access." |
| 18–25s | Character-led action: Alex moves task cards; Mia reacts as calendar and checklist elements update. No fake real-world app integrations are shown. | **Mia:** "For example, it could organise that checklist, check your calendar, and prepare a plan." |
| 25–32s | Mia pauses the animated task board with an open palm. Camera moves closer for emphasis. | **Mia:** "But it still needs the right permissions — and important actions should have safeguards." |
| 32–40s | Both characters face the audience. The chat bubble transforms into a task-flow icon. | **Alex:** "So, chatbot: answers. Agent: works toward a goal using tools." |
| 40–44s | Mia repeats the opening gesture; the first-frame composition returns for a loop. | **Mia:** "Now, what would you ask an agent to do?" |

## Production requirements

- Runtime target: 40–44 seconds.
- Format: 1080×1920, 9:16, 24 or 30 FPS.
- Character continuity: Alex and Mia must retain the same face, clothing, proportions, voice identity, and color palette in every shot.
- Performance: real body/hand/head movement, eye direction, facial expressions, and mouth movement synchronized to dialogue.
- Voice: two distinct, natural English voices; no eSpeak/robotic placeholder narration.
- Visual rhythm: a meaningful character, camera, prop, or scene change every 2–4 seconds; avoid random effects.
- Audio: clean dialogue first; restrained original/licensed music and subtle sound effects only if rights are clear.
- Captions: accurate, timed, inside vertical safe margins; no clipped text or literal escape characters.
- Claims: explain the general distinction without suggesting agents are autonomous or infallible. Tool access and actions depend on permissions and implementation.
